Hunting the Unfinished

In 2011, a PhD thesis at Concordia University closed dozens of doors in the theory of strongly regular graphs — and left three of them ajar, each marked with a single typeset character: ?. This book is about finding those question marks fifteen years later, and finishing what they started.

Majid Behbahani and Clement Lam invented the orbit-matrix method for hunting strongly regular graphs with prescribed automorphisms, and ran it across every open parameter set under 100 vertices. Where their tables say 0 graphs found, a case is closed. Where they say ?, the thesis is explicit: "we have not found any solutions and we were not able to finish the program due to time constraints." Three fixed-point-free cases carry that mark:

$\mathrm{srg}(85,30,11,10)$order $17$, no fixed points$2$?
$\mathrm{srg}(88,27,6,9)$order $11$, no fixed points$5$?
$\mathrm{srg}(99,42,21,15)$order $11$, no fixed points$173$?

Every one of these parameter sets is an open existence question: no graph with those parameters has ever been found, and no proof excludes one. Finishing a question-mark case has exactly two possible endings — a brand-new strongly regular graph, or a theorem that no such graph carries that symmetry. Either ending is worth having. This book records the pursuit: the archaeology that found the marks, the modern pipeline that can finish them, and the verdicts as they land.

Relation to the sibling books

This is the fourth book of a series. Hunting SRG(37), Hunting SRG(69) and Hunting SRG(99) each chase a single hard target; this one sweeps up the small, sharply-bounded cases the literature started and never ended. The tooling is shared — the same engines, the same auditors, the same rule that a timeout is never a theorem.
