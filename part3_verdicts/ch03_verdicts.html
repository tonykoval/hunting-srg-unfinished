3.1   $\mathrm{srg}(85,30,11,10)$, order $17$ — CLOSED: no order-17 automorphism

Status 2026-08-11. Enumeration settled: the two orbit matrices of Chapter 1, matching Behbahani–Lam's count exactly. Then the arithmetic stage (§2.1½) rewrote the battle map: the trace-$50$ matrix — the $\sim 2^{155}$ monster that three $12$-hour searches had timed out against — lifts to nothing, by the Galois multiplicity count ($16 \nmid 30$; one line, no compute). Fifteen years of "?" on that matrix, and it never needed a computer. The whole case now rides on the trace-$32$ matrix alone, under attack by the pseudo-Boolean encoder with the theorem-derived character-trace constraints wired in (every nonzero shift in exactly $2$ diagonal supports). The scoped prize is unchanged: since the $f=17$ row is already closed, an UNSAT here finishes no $\mathrm{srg}(85,30,11,10)$ admits an automorphism of order $17$ — and a SAT decodes to the first known graph with these parameters.

Status 2026-08-15. The solver road has now been walked to its end for this matrix: a full $12$-hour pseudo-Boolean attempt with both character-trace constraint families (linear diagonal counts and quadratic autocorrelation flatness) returned undecided, joining the plain-encoding timeouts before it. So the campaign switched instruments. The trace-$32$ matrix is now under a sharded, pruner-free backtracker: its first branching level has exactly $28$ choices, each shard owns one, and each shard's verdict is written to a ledger the moment it lands — progress that survives power cycles, which the earlier monolithic runs never did (three of them lost twelve hours apiece to shutdowns). Every shard that reaches EXHAUSTED is permanent; all $28$ exhausted with zero solutions is a refutation sound on its own, with no pruner and no solver in the chain of trust. The first six shards each survived their two-hour rung — expected for $p=17$, whose sub-trees are large — and will be re-attacked at four, eight and twenty-four hours. The honest position: the last matrix of this case is being exhausted shard by shard, and nothing has lifted.

Status 2026-08-19. All $28$ shards recorded at two hours, all undecided — and the numbers say why this matrix is a different animal from the $88$ pair: its shards reach only a few thousand nodes in two hours, against sixty million for $p=11$. The $p=17$ lift is not a large tree so much as a slow one; the cost per node, not the count of nodes, is the enemy, and no time-limit rung cures that. The next lever here is engineering — make the node cheaper — not hardware.

Theorem (2026-08-23, verdict shipped 2026-08-31)

No $\mathrm{srg}(85,30,11,10)$ admits an automorphism of order $17$. With Behbahani–Lam's Theorem 4.7, the possible prime divisors of its automorphism group are exactly $\{2,3,5\}$.

The charm85 run finished in $41$ hours on the $24$-core box: all $24$ shards report adm=0 with clean exits — every one of the $324{,}240$ canonical row-$0$ states of the trace-$32$ matrix has zero admissible extensions. Combined with the one-line Galois death of the trace-$50$ matrix and the closed $f=17$ row, the fifteen-year question mark of Behbahani–Lam is a period. The scoped prize of this chapter is won — by the spectral engine, not the backtracker it replaced.

3.1.1   charm85: the exact spectral closure run (2026-08-21)

The trace-$32$ matrix is now under a finite, exact attack — no more open-ended solver time-limits. The character-matrix engine built for the $88$ survivors (§3.2.1) turns out to fit this case far better, for one structural reason: here the character spectrum is $5^2(-4)^3$, so the projector has rank 2 — rows $2$–$4$ of every candidate are computed by linear algebra, never searched. The whole question is a two-row search space, and it is countable: after the gauge quotient ($17$-fold per row-$0$ block) and the Galois multiplier quotient ($\mathbb{Z}_{17}^{*}$, validated by brute-force orbit computation on $\mathrm{srg}(25)$), the root set is exactly 324{,}240 canonical row-$0$ states (enumerated in 158 s). Every root sampled so far — spanning three independent probe runs — has zero admissible row-$1$ extensions.

The production run (charm85) shards the roots across a $24$-core server (~$2.5$ days) with a $5$-shard home replica as backup; every shard verdict is a permanent ledger line, and the claim rule is strict: refutation requires admissible = 0 on all shards, while any dumped candidate decodes — by the proven full circle — to an actual $\mathrm{srg}(85,30,11,10)$. Context from the Behbahani thesis (Thm 4.7, independently re-verified this week): the full prime frontier for this parameter set is $\{2,3,5,17\}$ — so this run decides the last large prime. The diagonal predicate quoted above (every nonzero shift in exactly $2$ diagonal supports) is Ishida's fixed-point-free pin, and doubles as a free verification test for any candidate the run emits.

3.2   $\mathrm{srg}(88,27,6,9)$, order $11$ — two survivors, beyond exhaustion — measured

Status 2026-08-10. The re-count played out as a small drama in the pipeline's own terms. The first CP-SAT enumeration ran out of its hour with status FEASIBLE — incomplete — holding $4$ classes against Behbahani–Lam's $5$; under the claim discipline that comparison is forbidden, and rightly so. The Rust generator (full diagonals) then finished properly in two seconds: $5$ orbit matrices — matching Behbahani–Lam's $5$ exactly, the second fifteen-year count agreement of the campaign.

Status 2026-08-11. The arithmetic stage then cut the case to less than half its size: the Galois count kills every matrix off trace $30$ — three of the five lift to nothing by hand (traces $12$, $48$, $12$: $10 \nmid 52,\, 48,\, 52$). The trace-split re-enumeration is confirming the count slice by slice: trace-$48$ already OPTIMAL, $1 = 1$; traces $30$ and $12$ hold $\geq 2$ each with long-budget completion runs underway. The two survivors (both trace-$30$) are under the strengthened pseudo-Boolean attack. The April-era prediction that these matrices "won't lift" — made on machinery since shown incomplete — is now sixty per cent theorem and forty per cent open question, which is more honesty than it started the week with.

Status 2026-08-15. Both trace-$30$ survivors resisted their full-strength solver attempts — four hours each with the complete constraint stack, undecided — so both went to the sharded pruner-free backtracker, thirty-two shards apiece. The picture that emerged is clean: twenty of thirty-two shards exhausted on each matrix, zero solutions, banked permanently, with a hard core of eight shards per matrix that survive two hours and now climb the time-limit ladder. The four no-symmetry-breaking cross-checks of the three theorem-killed matrices also ran to their budgets undecided — which changes nothing (those matrices are dead by the Galois count regardless) but is recorded because this book records everything. If the hard cores yield at four or eight hours, $\mathrm{srg}(88,27,6,9)$'s order-$11$ question closes with a refutation that rests on nothing but exhaustive search and a one-page theorem — the first period this chapter would get to print.

Status 2026-08-19. The period did not come, and the reason is now a measurement rather than a guess. The two survivors were taken to a $24$-core server as a $10 imes10$ grid of depth-two cells — each cell a tenth of one of the home shards that had been failing at four hours. The first wave of $24$ cells came back $24$ of $24$ undecided at four hours. The tree profile explains it: thirty-five levels deep, branching ten at every level we measured, so even a depth-two cell holds more than $10^{10}$ effective nodes. Meanwhile every solver road had already been walked — three encodings (plain, character-trace, and a new one carrying the exact per-character spectrum as an annihilation identity over $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{11}]$) across three engine families, all undecided at two to twelve hours. The annihilation model is worth a sentence of its own: it refutes a Galois-dead matrix in nineteen seconds, yet cannot split a live one — which says, precisely, that the survivors' hardness lives inside the space satisfying every eigenvalue-derived condition we know how to write. They die by exhaustion or not at all, and exhaustion is out of reach for every fleet we have. Behbahani–Lam wrote "not able to finish the program due to time constraints"; fifteen years later, with sound sharding, four solvers and a theorem that killed three of their five matrices by hand, the remaining two stand for the same reason they stood then. The server went back to Conway's graph, where its hours buy certificates. The honest entry for this case is the one the book promised it would print when it had to: open, bounded, and measured — and waiting on mathematics, not machines.

3.2.1   The spectral tree, measured (2026-08-20)

The character-matrix reformulation (exact enumeration of Hermitian $C \in M_8(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{11}])$ with $(C-3I)(C+6I)=0$, one block-row per orbit) was pushed to a full meet-in-the-middle engine with a gauge quotient ($11$-fold per row-0 block), a Galois multiplier quotient ($\mathbb{Z}_{11}^{*}$, validated by brute-force orbit computation on srg(25)), and rank-$5$ completion (rows 5–7 solved, not searched). Probe measurements on $M_0$:

  • 83,336 canonical admissible row-0 states (28.6 s to enumerate);
  • $\approx$120,000 admissible row-1 extensions per root (measured exactly on a 1/1000 root sample: 19 roots → 2,281,399 pairs), i.e. the tree is $\sim 10^{10}$ wide at depth 2 of 8.

This is a $10^{25}$ compression of the raw $10^{35}$ block-labeling space, and it localizes the hardness exactly: the norm/Gram join between rows 1 and 2. It is also a quantitative strengthening of the verdict above: even granting every eigenvalue and projector constraint for free, an exhaustive traversal of $M_0$'s admissible tree must visit $\sim 10^{10}$ mid-depth states. Exhaustive closure of the two survivors by spectral enumeration is therefore out of reach on current hardware; the instrument survives as a width-measuring probe and as a 19-second refuter for Galois-dead matrices.

Frontier note (2026-08-21). The Behbahani thesis (Thm 4.9) pins the full prime frontier for $\mathrm{srg}(88,27,6,9)$ at $\{2,3,5,11\}$ — this week we re-verified the surrounding exclusions ($p = 17, 19, 23$) with an independent implementation, seconds each at the prototype layer. The measurement of §3.2.1 is thus the precise reason the order-$11$ case is the hard one: it is the only large prime the classical machinery leaves standing, and its spectral tree is $\sim 10^{10}$ wide.

3.3   $\mathrm{srg}(99,42,21,15)$, order $11$ — queued; order $7$ closed

Status 2026-08-10. The largest of the three ($173$ matrices over $9$ orbits). Queued behind the smaller cases; the same pipeline applies unchanged.

3.3.1   First blood: no automorphism of order $7$ (2026-08-21)

While the order-$11$ case waits its turn, the order-$7$ question — which Behbahani–Lam's Theorem 4.15 left open — is now closed:

Theorem

If an $\mathrm{srg}(99,42,21,15)$ exists, the only possible prime divisors of $|\mathrm{Aut}|$ are $2, 3, 5, 11$.

The proof is a complete orbit-matrix enumeration at $p=7$ over all admissible fixed-point counts $f \in \{1, 8, 15, 22, 29, 36\}$: every $f$ returns zero orbit matrices as a finished (non-timeout) search, run with the post-audit full-diagonal engine whose $f>0$ path passed positive controls (Petersen, both an $f=0$ and an $f=1$ known automorphism) the same day. An earlier run of the pre-audit engine had claimed this result; per this book's own rules it was treated as unproven until re-derived — the re-derivation confirmed it. One condition of the closing checklist below remains open before this is called finished: the independent-method double-confirmation (a CP-SAT enumeration of the same prototypes), which is queued.

Status update (2026-08-30): the order-$7$ theorem is CONDITIONAL

After the above was written, the Behbahani thesis full text surfaced a conflict: its Table 22 records $21{,}989$ orbit matrices at $(p,f) = (7,1)$, where our sound re-derivation finds \emph{zero}. One of the two enumerations is wrong — plausibly theirs (our engine's spectral prototype layer may simply be killing raw matrices their 2009 conditions admitted, and our CP-SAT partitions keep returning only INFEASIBLE where they close) — but per this book's own rules the theorem stays conditional until the raw-level partition finishes or the kill is derived by hand. Nothing is claimed until then.

3.3.2   The wall, measured — and $173$ becomes $2$ (2026-08-30)

The order-$11$ case itself moved decisively. Our sound enumeration finds $172$ orbit matrices (Behbahani–Lam report $173$; the $\pm 1$ is under arbitration and, as shown below, cannot touch the live part). The Galois multiplicity count — the same one-line arithmetic that killed the trace-$50$ matrix at $85$ — forces the quotient trace to be $30$, and the trace histogram $\{18\colon 1,\ 30\colon 2,\ 42\colon 14,\ 54\colon 44,\ 66\colon 99,\ 78\colon 12\}$ kills $170$ of the $172$ instantly: the fifteen-year wall of $173$ matrices was $99\%$ illusory — exactly two matrices ever mattered. Both survivors have character rank $2$, so the charm engine applies with rows $2$–$8$ computed rather than searched — but the measured cost is hours per root against $1.1$–$5.2$ million roots per matrix: the same wall class as the $88$ survivors (§3.2.1). The two matrices are recorded as the complete Galois-alive set; closing them awaits either a further quotient or new arithmetic.

How a section here gets to close

Three conditions, none waivable: every orbit matrix of the case carries an explicit EXHAUSTED, a hand-checkable refutation theorem (§2.1½), or a twice-verified lifted graph; the enumeration behind those matrices is double-confirmed; and the literature has been searched for anyone who finished the case since 2011. Then — and only then — the question mark becomes a period.
