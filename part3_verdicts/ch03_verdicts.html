3.1   $\mathrm{srg}(85,30,11,10)$, order $17$ — last matrix under sharded exhaustion

Status 2026-08-11. Enumeration settled: the two orbit matrices of Chapter 1, matching Behbahani–Lam's count exactly. Then the arithmetic stage (§2.1½) rewrote the battle map: the trace-$50$ matrix — the $\sim 2^{155}$ monster that three $12$-hour searches had timed out against — lifts to nothing, by the Galois multiplicity count ($16 \nmid 30$; one line, no compute). Fifteen years of "?" on that matrix, and it never needed a computer. The whole case now rides on the trace-$32$ matrix alone, under attack by the pseudo-Boolean encoder with the theorem-derived character-trace constraints wired in (every nonzero shift in exactly $2$ diagonal supports). The scoped prize is unchanged: since the $f=17$ row is already closed, an UNSAT here finishes no $\mathrm{srg}(85,30,11,10)$ admits an automorphism of order $17$ — and a SAT decodes to the first known graph with these parameters.

Status 2026-08-15. The solver road has now been walked to its end for this matrix: a full $12$-hour pseudo-Boolean attempt with both character-trace constraint families (linear diagonal counts and quadratic autocorrelation flatness) returned undecided, joining the plain-encoding timeouts before it. So the campaign switched instruments. The trace-$32$ matrix is now under a sharded, pruner-free backtracker: its first branching level has exactly $28$ choices, each shard owns one, and each shard's verdict is written to a ledger the moment it lands — progress that survives power cycles, which the earlier monolithic runs never did (three of them lost twelve hours apiece to shutdowns). Every shard that reaches EXHAUSTED is permanent; all $28$ exhausted with zero solutions is a refutation sound on its own, with no pruner and no solver in the chain of trust. The first six shards each survived their two-hour rung — expected for $p=17$, whose sub-trees are large — and will be re-attacked at four, eight and twenty-four hours. The honest position: the last matrix of this case is being exhausted shard by shard, and nothing has lifted.

Status 2026-08-15. The picture that emerged is clean: twenty of thirty-two shards exhausted on each matrix, zero solutions, banked permanently, with a hard core of eight shards per matrix that survive two hours and now climb the time-limit ladder. The four no-symmetry-breaking cross-checks of the three theorem-killed matrices also ran to their budgets undecided — which changes nothing (those matrices are dead by the Galois count regardless) but is recorded because this book records everything. If the hard cores yield at four or eight hours, $\mathrm{srg}(88,27,6,9)$'s order-$11$ question closes with a refutation that rests on nothing but exhaustive search and a one-page theorem — the first period this chapter would get to print.

How a section here gets to close

Three conditions, none waivable: every orbit matrix of the case carries an explicit EXHAUSTED, a hand-checkable refutation theorem (§2.1½), or a twice-verified lifted graph; the enumeration behind those matrices is double-confirmed; and the literature has been searched for anyone who finished the case since 2011. Then — and only then — the question mark becomes a period.
