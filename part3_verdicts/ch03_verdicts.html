3.1.1   charm85: the exact spectral closure run (2026-08-21)

The trace-$32$ matrix is now under a finite, exact attack — no more open-ended solver time-limits. The character-matrix engine built for the $88$ survivors (§3.2.1) turns out to fit this case far better, for one structural reason: here the character spectrum is $5^2(-4)^3$, so the projector has rank 2 — rows $2$–$4$ of every candidate are computed by linear algebra, never searched. The whole question is a two-row search space, and it is countable: after the gauge quotient ($17$-fold per row-$0$ block) and the Galois multiplier quotient ($\mathbb{Z}_{17}^{*}$, validated by brute-force orbit computation on $\mathrm{srg}(25)$), the root set is exactly 324{,}240 canonical row-$0$ states (enumerated in 158 s). Every root sampled so far — spanning three independent probe runs — has zero admissible row-$1$ extensions.

The production run (charm85) shards the roots across a $24$-core server (~$2.5$ days) with a $5$-shard home replica as backup; every shard verdict is a permanent ledger line, and the claim rule is strict: refutation requires admissible = 0 on all shards, while any dumped candidate decodes — by the proven full circle — to an actual $\mathrm{srg}(85,30,11,10)$. Context from the Behbahani thesis (Thm 4.7, independently re-verified this week): the full prime frontier for this parameter set is $\{2,3,5,17\}$ — so this run decides the last large prime. The diagonal predicate quoted above (every nonzero shift in exactly $2$ diagonal supports) is Ishida's fixed-point-free pin, and doubles as a free verification test for any candidate the run emits.

3.2.1   The spectral tree, measured (2026-08-20)

The character-matrix reformulation (exact enumeration of Hermitian $C \in M_8(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{11}])$ with $(C-3I)(C+6I)=0$, one block-row per orbit) was pushed to a full meet-in-the-middle engine with a gauge quotient ($11$-fold per row-0 block), a Galois multiplier quotient ($\mathbb{Z}_{11}^{*}$, validated by brute-force orbit computation on srg(25)), and rank-$5$ completion (rows 5–7 solved, not searched). Probe measurements on $M_0$:

  • 83,336 canonical admissible row-0 states (28.6 s to enumerate);
  • $\approx$120,000 admissible row-1 extensions per root (measured exactly on a 1/1000 root sample: 19 roots → 2,281,399 pairs), i.e. the tree is $\sim 10^{10}$ wide at depth 2 of 8.

This is a $10^{25}$ compression of the raw $10^{35}$ block-labeling space, and it localizes the hardness exactly: the norm/Gram join between rows 1 and 2. It is also a quantitative strengthening of the verdict above: even granting every eigenvalue and projector constraint for free, an exhaustive traversal of $M_0$'s admissible tree must visit $\sim 10^{10}$ mid-depth states. Exhaustive closure of the two survivors by spectral enumeration is therefore out of reach on current hardware; the instrument survives as a width-measuring probe and as a 19-second refuter for Galois-dead matrices.

Frontier note (2026-08-21). The Behbahani thesis (Thm 4.9) pins the full prime frontier for $\mathrm{srg}(88,27,6,9)$ at $\{2,3,5,11\}$ — this week we re-verified the surrounding exclusions ($p = 17, 19, 23$) with an independent implementation, seconds each at the prototype layer. The measurement of §3.2.1 is thus the precise reason the order-$11$ case is the hard one: it is the only large prime the classical machinery leaves standing, and its spectral tree is $\sim 10^{10}$ wide.

3.2.2   The period (2026-09-01)

The mathematics arrived eleven days after the paragraph above asked for it, and it was a single number. The two survivors share quotient trace $30$, and that trace fixes the character spectrum of every lift: $3^5(-6)^3$. Nobody had looked at the second exponent. It says that $G = 3I - C$ is a positive-semidefinite matrix of rank three in every embedding of $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{11}]$ — so three orbits carry the whole Gram geometry, and every other orbit's block is not searched but computed: its inner products with the frame are forced by an exact Schur identity over the ring, its diagonal by the exact-cover residual, and floating point enters only as a prefilter that is re-checked exactly. This is the same idea that closed §3.1 (there the rank was two); the $88$ pair had been attacked for a month by engines that traversed the $10^{10}$-wide tree of §3.2.1 instead of noticing that the tree has rank three. The new engine (--frame3) was validated the way the rank-two one was: dump-identical solution sets against the default engine on seventeen matrices across six parameter sets — including $\mathrm{srg}(40,12,2,4)/\mathbb{Z}_5$, which has exactly the $88$ rank shape — and must-find controls on Paley$(25)$ and the Paley$(49)$ quotient. A second cut came from symmetry: the orbit permutations that preserve the quotient matrix act on its lifts, and the second survivor has $240$ of them, which shrank its root set from $426{,}804$ to $2{,}550$.

Verdict. Both matrices, four shards each, every shard admissible = 0 with clean exit; dump files empty. Wall time on four cores of the home box: six and a half minutes — two for the first matrix ($42{,}208$ roots), four for the second — against the months of undecided solver hours, the $24$-of-$24$ undecided grid cells, and Behbahani–Lam's own "not able to finish". The chain is short enough to print in full: an order-$11$ automorphism fixes $f \le 33$ points with $11 \mid f$, and since $11 > \max(\lambda,\mu)$ the fixed subgraph must be empty, a single vertex, $K_8$, or an $\mathrm{srg}(f,k',6,9)$ — the three $f>0$ options fail integrality, so $f = 0$; the orbit matrix is then one of exactly five (our count, their count); the Galois multiplicity count kills three by hand; and the rank-three engine finds no character matrix for the other two. Hence:

Theorem (2026-09-01)

No strongly regular graph with parameters $(88,27,6,9)$ admits an automorphism of order $11$. The prime frontier of Behbahani–Lam's Theorem 4.9 tightens from $\{2,3,5,11\}$ to $\{2,3,5\}$.

Evidence grade, stated plainly. This is a single-engine verdict, resting on the validation battery above rather than on an independent method applied to these two matrices. The "twenty-two of thirty-two shards exhausted" of the backtracker ledgers is not corroboration — those shards were empty slices (the caveat of 2026-08-19), and the ten live shards per matrix never finished. Two hardening runs follow the same rule as §3.1's re-verification: the engine re-run without the automorphism quotient over the full multiplier root set ($83{,}336$ and $426{,}804$ roots; the first matrix is already clean, $4/4$), and the default row-by-row engine on a root slice as a cross-check. Their ledger lines are recorded in the repository (out/miqcp/charm88_f3*). The second of the three question marks is closed; the third (§3.3.3) is one matrix from joining it.

3.3.4   Order $3$: the fixed-point range collapses (2026-09-08)

Behbahani–Lam ran no $p=3$ rows for this parameter set at all, so its order-$3$ case had thirteen candidate fixed-point counts, $f = 0, 3, \dots, 36$, each a separate enumeration, and the two largest had just burnt four hours each without finishing. Two pieces of algebra removed eight of them by hand. On the character block $C$ of an order-$3$ automorphism the identity $(C-rI)(C-sI)=0$ gives $G^2 = dG$ for $G = rI - C$ ($d = r - s = 12$), whose diagonal says that a row with $a_{ii} = 0$ has exactly $27$ off-diagonal entries in $\{1,2\}$ and a row with $a_{ii} = 2$ exactly $20$. With $o = (99-f)/3 \le 27$ orbits no row of the first kind fits, so for $18 \le f \le 36$ every diagonal is $2$, every $C_{ii}$ is $\omega + \omega^2 = -1$, and the trace of $C$ against its spectrum $\{9^m, (-3)^{o-m}\}$ forces $12m = 2o$: only $o = 24$, i.e. $f = 27$, survives; $f \ge 39$ admits no row type at all. The controls are the known graphs: the true GQ$(3,5)$ and Paley$(81)$ quotients sit exactly where the identity predicts, $T(9)$'s three order-$3$ actions pass and its three impossible fixed-point counts fail, with the generator agreeing on all of them.

The last row, $f = 27$, has a character block of rank $4$ — $24$ vectors of norm $\sqrt 2$ in $\mathbb{C}^4$ with pairwise inner products of modulus $0$ or $1$ and a tight-frame identity — small enough to decide without ever writing an orbit matrix. A CP-SAT model over the exact Eisenstein arithmetic (basis Gram classes, inner-product vectors as columns, frame budgets as integer equations, every constraint a necessary condition) returned INFEASIBLE for all $100$ basis classes in $117$ seconds, after finding feasible blocks on rank-$1$ to rank-$4$ controls where they exist ($L_2(7)$ with a rank-$4$ action among them) and none where they do not.

Lemma (2026-09-08)

An automorphism of order $3$ of a strongly regular graph with parameters $(99,42,21,15)$ fixes $0$, $3$, $6$, $9$, $12$ or $15$ vertices.

Evidence grade. A hand argument with positive and negative computational controls, plus a computational proof by a new tool that has not yet been re-derived by a second implementation; it does not touch the order-$11$ question of §3.3.3 and does not by itself decide the order-$3$ case — it says where that case lives: six rows, all with at least $28$ orbits. One more thing fell out of the controls: the fixed-point bound our frontier survey had been quoting, $f \le \max(\lambda,\mu)\,n/(k-s)$, is false ($T(10)$ has a $3$-cycle fixing $21 > 20$ pairs); the order-$11$ theorem for srg$(100,33,8,12)$ in §3.4 had cited it for "$f \in \{1,12,23\}$" and was re-checked the same evening — the larger $f$ admit no orbit-row prototype at all, so it stands on that firmer ground.

3.3.5   Order $5$: closed (2026-09-14)

An automorphism of order $5$ fixes $f \equiv 4 \pmod 5$ vertices and has $o = (99-f)/5$ orbits of length $5$. The order-$3$ algebra of §3.3.4 transfers to $p = 5$ with one change: the character block now lives over $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_5]$, and the trace identity that pinned $o = 24$ at $p = 3$ here pins the rank $a$ of the block and the diagonal-type counts $(n_0, n_2, n_4)$ from $f$ alone. Its counting half kills every row with $f \ge 54$. A second hand lemma, the row-capacity bound — each orbit row must distribute its degree over the $o - 1$ other orbits, and each diagonal type needs a minimum $o$ to do so ($19$, $16$, $16$, $15$ for the four types of this graph) — kills $f = 29, 34, 39, 44, 49$, where $o \le 14$, and inside the rows $f = 9, 14, 19$ every case with a type-$0$ row. Both lemmas were checked on the $90$ order-$5$ quotients of graphs that exist ($188$ row-type checks, $60$ of them tight).

What the lemmas leave — the rank-$3$ cases of $f = 9, 14, 19, 24$, one rank-$4$ case at $f = 19$, and the ten rank-$4$ cases of $f = 4$ — went to the basis-class engine: enumerate the Gram classes of a basis of the frame, and for each class let CP-SAT pick the $o$ columns of the block from the finitely many inner-product vectors the class admits, with the frame identity $G^2 = 12G$ as exact integer equations in four coordinates. Every class of every case returned INFEASIBLE. The largest row, $f = 4$, is ten cases with $20{,}652$ to $337{,}637$ classes each, split between the home box and the server. Nine classes on the server came back "UNKNOWN" and stayed so after a two-hour re-decide; the logs showed each verdict took $0.6$ seconds. They were not hard instances but a size guard in the engine (more than $25{,}000$ candidate columns), which returned before the pruning that would have decided them. With the guard moved after the pruning, one class fell to CP-SAT in $432$ seconds and the other eight to the degree pruning alone: no candidate column had enough compatible partners.

Theorem (2026-09-14)

A strongly regular graph with parameters $(99,42,21,15)$ has no automorphism of order $5$.

Evidence grade. Single engine. The two hand lemmas are controlled on every known order-$5$ quotient; the engine passes its must-find suite ($88$ of $88$ known frames found, $140$ of $140$ true bases recognised, re-run after the guard fix) but has not been re-derived by a second implementation, and the $f = 4$ row has no independent decision (the orbit-matrix generator reports zero prototypes there in two seconds, which is not quoted as evidence until its own control of that shape has run). The frontier of this parameter set is now $\{2, 3\}$, plus the conditional order-$7$ result of §3.3.1 and the single order-$11$ matrix of §3.3.3, which was still lifting on the server as this was written. A lesson for the ledgers goes with it: an UNKNOWN that returns in under a second is a verdict about the tool, not the instance — read the elapsed time before buying a longer limit.

3.4   $\mathrm{srg}(100,33,8,12)$, order $11$ — CLOSED CONDITIONALLY (see the 2026-09-17 note)

Status update (2026-09-17): this theorem is CONDITIONAL

One step below — “the row $f = 1$ has exactly $18$ orbit matrices” — is a count made by our orbit-matrix generator on a row with a fixed point. On 2026-09-17 that generator was caught returning a complete zero on four order-$3$ actions with fixed points of graphs that exist (the smallest: the $5 \times 5$ lattice graph with a $3$-cycle on its columns). Its counts on rows with fixed points are therefore not evidence until the defect is fixed. The independent solver run for this row stopped at “feasible” with $13$ classes, all among the $18$, which is reassuring but is not a completeness proof. What stands today: no automorphism of order $11$ whose $f = 1$ quotient is one of the $18$ known matrices; the rows $f \ge 23$ are dead by a hand argument (characters and row prototypes), and $f = 12$ by an independent solver proof. The order-$11$ results for $(99,42,21,15)$, $(85,30,11,10)$ are not affected: all their rows with fixed points die by the same hand argument.

Status 2026-09-02. This case was not on the map above until the Brouwer table was re-read verbatim: the list this book started from was stale, and $(100,33,8,12)$ — open, with Behbahani–Lam's Theorem 4.16 leaving $\{2,3,5,11\}$ as the possible prime orders and no table at all for the parameter set in the thesis — was the one open target below $100$ vertices nobody had run a single orbit-matrix row for. The rank survey of §3.2.2 found it at once: an automorphism of order $11$ fixes $f \equiv 100 \equiv 1 \pmod{11}$ points, the Behbahani bound $f \le \max(\lambda,\mu)\,n/(k-s) = 1200/40 = 30$ leaves $f \in \{1,12,23\}$, and for $f=1$ the quotient has $o = 9$ orbits and trace $30$, which forces the character spectrum $3^6(-7)^3$ of every lift. The second exponent is again a three: $G = 3I - C$ is positive semidefinite of rank three, the exact shape the engine of §3.2.2 was built for.

Enumeration. $f=12$ and $f=23$ have no orbit matrix at all — CP-SAT proves both rows infeasible in $6.9$ and $29.7$ seconds, independently of the Rust generator (whose own zero on the $f=12$ row would not have been admissible as evidence: that row took the route that was found unsound on 2026-09-02). The $f=1$ row has exactly $18$ orbit matrices, counted by the full-diagonal backtracking generator, re-derived class-exactly after the generator repairs of 2026-09-02 ($18 = 18$), and cross-checked by CP-SAT, whose one-hour run found $13$ classes, every one of them among the $18$ and none outside. The one imperfection is that the CP-SAT run ended FEASIBLE, not OPTIMAL, so the $18$ rests on the generator's completeness (audited by the must-find harness) plus a consistent lower bound, not on two complete counts.

The closure run (2026-09-02 to 2026-09-07). The rank-three frame engine in its fixed-point mode (--f 1 --mult --aut --frame3), one sequential run for the first matrix ($1{,}455{,}171$ roots, seven hours) and three shards for each of the other seventeen, at low priority on the home box between other lanes: fifty-one of fifty-one shards exit clean with admissible = 0, all dump files empty; $47$ million roots, $8\times10^8$ nodes, about $136$ CPU-hours (the last matrix twice, its first run lost to a shutdown). The ledger lines CHARM100_M0..M17_ALL_SHARDS_DONE adm_total=0 and the per-shard EXIT=0 lines are in out/miqcp/charm100_f1/. Hence:

Theorem (2026-09-07)

No strongly regular graph with parameters $(100,33,8,12)$ admits an automorphism of order $11$. The prime frontier of Behbahani–Lam's Theorem 4.16 tightens from $\{2,3,5,11\}$ to $\{2,3,5\}$.

Evidence grade. The same as §3.2.2, stated the same way: a single-engine verdict on the eighteen matrices, resting on the frame engine's validation battery (dump-identical solution sets against the default engine on seventeen matrices across six parameter sets; the fixed-point mode checked on the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ quotients of Petersen, $T(8)$ and $\mathrm{srg}(16,6,2,2)$, where it must find and does find the known lifts; the symmetry cut checked by the §3.2 no-symmetry re-runs). The natural hardening job is the same re-run without the symmetry quotient — the quotient matrices here have tiny automorphism groups (order $2$ for the last one), so it costs about twice the closure and belongs on a remote box, not on the critical path. With this the fourth of the book's cases gets its period, and every open parameter set below $100$ vertices now has prime frontier $\subseteq \{2,3,5\}$ except $\mathrm{srg}(99,42,21,15)$, whose order-$11$ case (§3.3.3) is the last large prime standing. (It fell on 2026-09-15; see §3.3.3.)
