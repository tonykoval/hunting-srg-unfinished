Chapter 5 · Part IV

The Iron Law and Other Hard-Won Rules

A year of hunting strongly regular graphs distils to a handful of rules. Each was paid for — in wasted core-hours, in claims that had to be retracted before anyone read them, or in bugs that waited months to be caught. They are stated here in full because the question-mark cases are exactly where all of them converge.

5.1   The iron law

The iron law of prescribed-symmetry hunting

Prescribed-symmetry search reaches only highly symmetric graphs, and at small vertex counts those catalogs are already complete — so it finds nothing new there. The unknown strongly regular graphs, where they exist at all, are overwhelmingly low-symmetry or large, which is precisely where prescribed symmetry cannot look.

This law is why roughly $950{,}000$ genuinely new strongly regular graphs were constructed by this project at incomplete-catalog parameters (via switching cascades seeded on low-automorphism graphs) while the open-existence parameters yielded none. The question-mark cases are the honest exception the law allows: there the prescribed-symmetry search is not a hunting strategy but a bounded question — does a $\mathbb{Z}_{17}$- or $\mathbb{Z}_{11}$-symmetric example exist, yes or no — and bounded questions can be finished. Nobody should expect a graph behind these doors; the law says the likely ending is a small clean theorem. But the doors are finite, and finishable, and fifteen years overdue.

5.2   The incompleteness saga

The single most consequential bug in the project's history was quiet: for primes $p \geq 5$, the orbit-matrix generator capped diagonal entries at $\{0, p{-}1\}$, silently discarding every matrix with an intermediate internal valency. Three separate times it produced a confident "No orbit matrices found" on cases where matrices exist — once live on $\mathrm{srg}(85,30,11,10)$ itself. The archaeology that exposed it was pure test hygiene: the fully-catalogued $\mathrm{srg}(25,12,5,6)$ under $\mathbb{Z}_5$ must have exactly $2$ orbit matrices; the old engine returned $1$, and nine regression tests had been faithfully pinning the wrong number for months. (Both of those $25$-vertex matrices, incidentally, lift — verified end-to-end by the modern pipeline.) Every "zero matrices" claim from the capped era had to be re-derived; the era's teaching is Chapter 2's refrain: an enumeration you have not cross-checked is a conjecture with a progress bar.

The same hygiene later paid twice more: the composite-order unlock (odd $q$ lifts route through the prime search, validated on $T(9)$ under $\mathbb{Z}_9$) — whose solution counts are now flagged for re-audit after the pruner affair of §2.3 — and the discovery that the lift's default pruner could erase a whole isomorphism class while stamping the search complete. Every instrument in this observatory has lied at least once. The pipeline assumes nothing else.

5.3   What the machines that talk are actually for

This project runs inside a collaboration with reasoning models, and it measured the obvious question early: can a language model solve these problems — construct a graph, crack an open case? The answer, tested against every instance that mattered, was no. The productive roles were narrower and better: deriving encodings, proposing symmetry arguments, and manufacturing small theorems that gate the compute. The measurement aged well. The Galois multiplicity filter of §2.1½ — a page of classical character counting that killed four of seven open matrices and then sharpened the solver's constraints on the survivors — is exactly that pattern executing at full value: the model did not out-search the solver; it noticed the search was unnecessary.

5.4   The filter travels

The arithmetic stage is not bespoke to the three question marks. Run across the project's other prime-order caches the same afternoon it was derived, it killed $4$ of $6$ enumerated matrices for $\mathrm{srg}(115,18,1,3)$ under $\mathbb{Z}_{23}$ — a case outside Behbahani–Lam's tables — and it makes a standing prediction for the largest question mark: of the $173$ orbit matrices awaiting re-enumeration for $\mathrm{srg}(99,42,21,15)$ under $\mathbb{Z}_{11}$, only those of trace $30$ can survive. When that enumeration lands, the prediction gets tested in public, in Chapter 3. A rule that risks nothing forecasts nothing.
