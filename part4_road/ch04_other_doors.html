Chapter 4 · Part IV

Every Other Door

Before this project walked back to Behbahani–Lam's question marks, it spent months trying to reach the same graphs through every other entrance it could find. None worked. This chapter records those attempts honestly — because each failure has a shape, and the shapes are why the orbit-matrix road, for these particular cases, is the only one with a visible end.

4.1   The design door: $S(2,6,51)$

The parameter set $\mathrm{srg}(85,30,11,10)$ has a beautiful back entrance. A Steiner system $S(2,6,51)$ — $85$ blocks of size $6$ on $51$ points, every pair of points in exactly one block — would have a block graph equal to $\mathrm{srg}(85,54,33,36)$, whose complement has exactly the open parameters. Build the design, win the graph. And the design question permits something the prescribed-symmetry road never can: a solution with trivial automorphism group.

Three engines went through that door. A Kramer–Mesner engine (prescribe a symmetry of the design, solve the orbit incidence system exactly) validated itself on the Fano plane and $PG(2,3)$, then met the same ceiling as everything else at the real target: with a multiplier of order $17$ the system reduces to two row-profiles, but the exact cover behind them is cluster-scale. A simulated-annealing constructor with two independent state spaces — one keeping all block sizes at $6$, one keeping point replications exact — drove the pair-coverage defect to $87$ but never to $0$. A GPU large-neighbourhood search rebuilt the annealer around an exhaustive $128{,}520$-move descent with ruin-and-repair; it reached defect $79$, the best ever, and then proved its own plateau was structural: $230{,}000$ sideways moves with the full neighbourhood exhausted, all $1{,}392$ small ruins refuted exactly, and an orphan-pair argument showing the remaining defect cannot be repaired locally at all. The door is not closed — but every local method now provably stalls in front of it.

4.2   The cyclotomic door

In 2021 Gritsenko settled a neighbouring open case, $\mathrm{srg}(65,32,15,16)$, with a block-circulant construction over cyclotomic classes. Porting the recipe to $n=85$ was attempted here: the five-step pipeline was rebuilt end to end, and then stopped by arithmetic — the final linear-reduction step needs a derivation the $65$ case never required, because the relevant cyclotomic polynomial ($\Phi_{21}$) is irreducible where the smaller case conveniently factored. The port sits unfinished, its wall documented, its arithmetic bug on the ledger. No verdict; a lesson in how little constructions generalize.

4.3   The two Aprils of $\mathrm{srg}(88,27,6,9)$

This parameter set carries both the project's proudest early theorem and its most instructive false one — from the same month.

The real one and the void one

Real: no $\mathrm{srg}(88,27,6,9)$ admits an automorphism of order $7$ — every admissible fixed-point count closed by two independent engines (native enumeration and a pseudo-Boolean solver), verdicts in agreement, written up with runtimes. Void: the same month's log also recorded a "$147$-million-node exhaustive lift" claiming no order-$11$ symmetry — over the two orbit matrices the generator of the era could see. The true count, established fifteen years earlier and re-derived here in Chapter 3, is five. A perfectly exhaustive search of an incomplete list proves nothing, at any node count. The order-$11$ question is precisely what Part III is finishing properly.

4.4   What the doors have in common

Local search stalls on structure it cannot see; exact cover explodes past cluster scale; constructions do not transfer; and exhaustive searches inherit every defect of their inputs. Against that, the question-mark cases have a property no other door offers: a finite, enumerable, twice-checkable to-do list — a handful of orbit matrices, each of which must either lift or be refuted. Chapter 2's pipeline (and the arithmetic stage that later killed most of the list outright) exists because finiteness, in this business, is the rarest luxury there is.
